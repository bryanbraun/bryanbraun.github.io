title: "Not the End of the World"
author: Hannah Ritchie
date: 2026-10-13
categories: non-fiction
rating: 4/5
---


In this book, Hannah Ritchie makes a case that environmental sustainability is not only possible, but achievable within a single generation. To do this, Hannah Ritchie channels Hans Rosling, wielding data to show that humanity has made great progress on many concerning issues, and has a path to address the ones that remain unsolved. One thing she does in the book is take clickbait environmentalist headlines and investigate the data to check their accuracy (some are close, but many are not). To be clear, she's not a climate-change denialist. There are many topics where the data points to heavy costs—for example, beef stands out as an incredibly inefficient source of protein, contributing to both deforestation and climate change at significantly higher rates than pork, chicken, and other alternatives. But many other common environmentalist practices (think: local-first, recycling, plastic use, etc.) fail to stand under scrutiny. The book felt like a breath of fresh air in the environmentalist media landscape we find ourself in and I hope more people read it. If you want a taste of the ethos behind it, check out this essay from Our World in Data (her employer): [The world is awful. The world is much better. The world can be much better](https://ourworldindata.org/much-better-awful-can-be-better).
