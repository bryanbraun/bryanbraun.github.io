title: "Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art"
author: Scott McCloud
date: 2026-09-24

rating: 4/5

I've seen this book recommended many times and I finally got hold of a physical copy and gave it a read. Pretty quickly, I was able to see what made it special. It was like a textbook covering the history and artform of comics, with the book itself being written in the form of a comic. This format allowed for a lot of creativity in explaining the material, which the author took full advantage of. For example, when the narrator began explaining realism, the appearance of his own character transitioned between the different levels of realism as he spoke, letting the reader see and feel the difference. This kind of thing was happening constantly—it was clever, effective, and a perfect demonstration of the author's central point that comics have more to offer than most people think (given their long-standing connection to cheap entertainment and advertising). Maybe this isn't such a radical message today, given the recent proliferation of graphic novels (including non-fiction ones), but this book was written in 1994! I have no doubt that this book's contributions helped build the graphic novel ecosystem that flourishes today.
