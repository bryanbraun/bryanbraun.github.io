title: "Super Mario: How Nintendo Conquered America"
author: Jeff Ryan
date: 2026-11-XX
categories: non-fiction
rating: 4/5
---

This book gives a comprehensive history of Nintendo of America, from it's humble beginnings selling Donkey Kong Arcade cabinets in the 80s into the console wars of the 2000s (at least through 2011 when the book came out). We were a Nintendo house growing up, so it was fun to hear the stories behind the consoles and games I loved to play as a kid. The book's author is more of a journalist than a Nintendo fan, which contributed to some mistakes (describing one game as "Mario's Party" instead of "Mario Party") and oversights (he seemed to gloss over Pokemon, which I think deserved way more attention than it was given). That said, the author did a great job at getting to the heart of what makes Nintendo special: they are a team of perfectionists who care deeply about delivering fun games, and are unafraid to use their past success as leverage to stay in the game. You can see it in IP licensing, their re-release strategy, and their battles for survival against Sega and the Playstation 2. I could imagine a better book on this topic (this one is 15 years old already), but this one was pretty good.
