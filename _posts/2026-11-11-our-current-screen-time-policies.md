Managing a house with five kids can be challenging so I like to share what's working for us when I can. This post is how we currently handle screen time.

Screens can be addictive, and it's easy for anyone (both kids and adults) to fall into unhealthy patterns using them. Historically, I've been uncomfortable with screen time restrictions because I don't want to restrict all the useful, creative, and productive opportunities that screens can provide. We've tried zero restrictions in the past (alongside [Daily Demos]({{site.url}}/2019/07/16/daily-demos-a-ritual-for-raising-kids-that-create/) as a creativity incentive), which worked ok, but at some point we wanted bit more structure. Here's what that structure currently looks like.

## Device use categories

For family members without phones, we have carved out the following categories of time:

* **"Free Time"** - Kids are free to do whatever activities they want (bounded only by what we've made available for them at the house). Video games, movies, board games, homework, run around  outside, whatever. Often, this time trends towards consumption on devices, but it doesn't have to. We call it "free time" instead of "screen time," because we want to emphasize that the opportunities are wider than just screens.
* **"Project Time"** - Kids are encouraged to do projects, which are usually defined as creative activities of all kinds (with or without screens). This category can be blurry so we've often put together lists of approved project time activities, including things like: arts and crafts, coding (including "[Scratch create](https://scratch.mit.edu/)"), playing musical instruments, recording videos, Minecraft (creative mode), [the Powder Toy]({{site.url}}/2021/01/18/the-powder-toy/), [Dojo Islands](https://www.classdojo.com/dojo-islands/), and anything on [Let's Get Creative]({{site.url}}/2023/10/10/lets-get-creative/). Device-based consumption is not allowed. Offline activities are usually considered fine (reading a book, doing a puzzle, etc).
* **"No devices"** - No device use allowed.

**Addendum: Grant Proposals**

If a kid wants a one-time exception to one of the categories above, we tell them to "submit a grant proposal." This means they need to get some paper, and write out what they want to do, and why they think it is justified.

* Example 1: *"I want to play Minecraft creative after dinner because I am building a full-scale piano, I want to demo it tonight, and I only have 10 more minutes of work until it's done."*
* Example 2: *"I want to play Terraria during project time because I am making an tutorial on how to beat Moon Lord, and I need to collect screenshots from the game for my tutorial."*

We usually approve grant proposals, as long as they aren't incomplete.

## Summary

Addictive device consumption can easily crowd out activities that are important long-term but deliver less dopamine short-term. Our device-time categories create a protected space for those activities on a consistent schedule that our kids can expect and make plans around. This is what works for us. Your milage may vary!
