I've had "AI usage statements" in the back of my mind ever since I read [Ethan Marcott's blog post sharing a round-up of his favorite ones](https://ethanmarcotte.com/wrote/folded-between-the-pages/). I didn't consider writing one for this site, since it had the smell of status-signaling. Like a quasi-political declaration of allegiance to some sort of in-group (be it pro-AI, anti-AI, etc). I couldn't be less interested in that.<sup id="fnref-1"><a href="#fn-1" class="footnote-ref">1</a></sup>

But then something happened that caught me completely off-guard.

I was reading a post on my friend's personal blog. This was a guy that I knew personally and looked up to. I've been reading everything he had been writing for years.

About two-thirds of the way through a long (and fairly insightful) blog post, I had a sudden realization that the post had been written by AI. I'm not the best at detecting this kind of thing, but once I saw it, I couldn't unsee it. It was unmistakable.

And the thing is, I didn't feel indifferent or even disappointed, as I might have predicted. I felt **betrayed**.

For somebody who considers themselves open-minded—even optimistic—towards the development of AI, I didn't know where this feeling was coming from. I have theories... maybe it has something to do with [the social contract between the writer and the reader](the social contract between writer and reader). Whatever it is, I didn't want anyone who was reading my blog (hi mom 👋) to feel the same thing.

So for the sake of transparency, here is my AI usage statement:

> The content of this website is written by me. I do not use AI to generate the text you are reading because writing from scratch helps me process my thoughts (which is one of the main reasons I blog in the first place). I don't currently use AI-powered writing tools (grammar-check, spell-check, dictation tools, etc), but I may experiment with them as long as they don't undermine the thinking process.
>
> The code for this website was originally hand-written, but I have since used AI to make several useful updates. I expect to continue using AI to write code for this website and other personal coding projects. When I create a public project exclusively with AI, I mention that fact in the project README.<sup id="fnref-2"><a href="#fn-2" class="footnote-ref">2</a></sup>

I am experimenting and my AI usage may change. When it does, I'll update the policy [here on my `/about` page]({{site.url}}/about/#ai-usage), which is where it will live long-term.
